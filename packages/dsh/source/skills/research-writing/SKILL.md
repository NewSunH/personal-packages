# Research and writing

Use an evidence-first workflow.

1. Clarify the research question, intended audience, deliverable, scope, and citation style when they materially affect the result.
2. Search broadly enough to map the topic, then prefer primary sources: official documentation, standards, datasets, original papers, and first-party announcements.
3. Record each source's title, author or organization, publication date, URL or DOI, and access date while researching. Never invent a citation, page number, DOI, quote, or result.
4. Keep three things visibly separate in notes and drafts: source-supported facts, your inference or synthesis, and open uncertainty.
5. For PDFs, extract text locally before analysis. Check tables, equations, figures, footnotes, and page boundaries against the original because text extraction can scramble them.
6. Build a claim-to-source outline before prose. Cite as close as practical to the supported claim and make clear when multiple sources disagree.
7. Draft for the requested audience, then run a final pass for unsupported claims, stale facts, citation mismatches, duplicated ideas, terminology drift, and overconfident wording.

When quoting, use only the minimum excerpt needed and preserve its meaning. If a source cannot be opened or verified, say so and do not represent it as checked.
